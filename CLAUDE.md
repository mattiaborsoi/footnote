# Footnote

A walk designed around you. A person describes the walk they are in the mood for; the app returns real stops from open map data, each with a short story and a "why it's for you" line, routed with time, distance and steps, exportable to Google Maps. Next.js 16 + TypeScript. **Claude curates, it never invents**: it only picks and orders stops from candidate IDs we supply and writes from the facts attached to them.

## Product

**Problem.** Asking a chatbot for a day out gives untailored waypoints with no timeline and no map. Footnote turns a person's dates, time, company and interests into a plan they can see on a map and trust the timings of: a desktop to plan, a phone to walk.

**Who.** Travellers with a free afternoon or a trip to plan, locals wanting somewhere new, anyone with ninety minutes to fill. Non-technical, mostly on phones.

**Request, never a blank box.** Six questions: where (start by current location or search; end the same, elsewhere, or "surprise me") · how long (slider with the implied distance) · who is walking (just me, kids, older people, limited mobility, pram: drives pace, breaks and step-free routing; pace stays overridable) · interests (icon tiles, long-press to rank: nature, modern architecture, history, technology, gaming, art and craft, cafés, views, photo spots, film locations, museums, shops) · when (now, or a day and time: unlocks opening hours and weather) · anything else (one optional free-text line, a constraint, never the sole input). Desktop shows all six on one page with a live summary sentence.

**Content.** Open data (OSM, Wikidata, Wikipedia) anchors real places. The **scout layer** is the content: a background job per city searches the web (blogs, travel sites, forums, video transcripts) for recommended spots, matches each to a real map place and stores name, one-line reason, source link and date. Facts and links only, never an author's text; every stop credits its source. It starts with key world capitals and expands to a city when people search for it. Per city, pre-made walks and must-sees are cached and offered first: a cached walk costs no model time; re-checking opening hours and weather for the person's date is free, tailoring it to their preferences uses the model.

**Repeat searches.** The same request within the same hour (start, end, duration, companions, interests, notes, language, normalised) is served from the stored walk instead of the model: a fingerprint column on `walks`, a 24-hour lookup, an event `walk.cache_hit`. Tailoring always builds fresh.

**Curation rules.** No story, no stop: a thin area gives a shorter walk, never a padded one. Opening hours are checked for the planned day and arrival time; closed stops are swapped or flagged. Weather nudges parks and views. Companions change dwell time, breaks and steps.

**Map.** One calm base map, desaturated while a walk shows. The route is one solid ink-blue line with a white casing. Stops are white numbered circles with a thin halo in the interest colour; start a square, end a diamond. Time pills per leg when zoomed in, one total pill otherwise, never a time on a pin. Detours: dashed blue at reduced opacity with lettered hollow pins, off by default on phone, behind one "Options" chip. "20 more minutes" extensions: a dotted amber spur with a tag, one at a time, tap to swap in. A closed stop gets a grey halo and a clock badge. Nothing thinner than 2 px, no colour-only distinctions.

**The wait (15 to 40 s).** While the model chooses, the server routes from the start to the twelve best candidates (the `probing` stage) and the map plays them like a trips layer: a bright head with a fading tail and a soft glow runs along each street route, four at a time, every batch a different set of destinations in a different palette, until the stops are chosen; reduced motion shows one still batch.  Four honest stages with real durations and no fake percentages: understood · searching · choosing stops · walking the route. On the map the search circle breathes out, candidate dots fade in, the chosen ones grow into numbered pins in walk order, the line draws itself. One short fact rotates underneath. After 20 s say it is slow; after 30 s offer "show what we have".

**Stories.** Name only on the map. One hook line of at most 90 characters on the card, expanding to three short paragraphs, then "why it's here for you" with the person's interests bolded and sources linked. Audio reading comes later.

**Around me (phone).** One button: location, 45 minutes, saved interests, now. The result is a fan of five options within ten minutes, swipeable cards with walking time and open-till, dashed spokes on the map. "Go" routes one leg; "Make this a walk" runs the full pipeline.

**Walks are pages.** Every walk has an id and a shareable link, cached on the device with its stories so it reads offline on the pavement. The **footnoted walk** is the signature: as you walk, numbered superscripts land where you paused, took a photo or opened a story, and at the end a one-page footnoted record of the walk can be shared as an image or printed.

**Language.** The interface and every story are in the person's language: the request carries `language` (default from the browser), the model writes in it, and facts come from that language's Wikipedia where it exists. English first, structure for more from day one.

**Product logs.** We must see what people ask for, how long they wait at each stage, what they tap and every error they meet. Events go to our own Postgres, server-side, keyed by walk and session id, with no third-party trackers and no analytics cookie, so no consent banner is needed. The free-text line is kept for product improvement with a 90-day retention and is named in the privacy notice; stage timings, model usage and scrubbed errors are kept as aggregates.

**Feedback button.** A bug icon on every screen. Reports go to a feedback table in our Postgres with the context attached automatically (walk id, screen, device class, the last few events) and an email notification to the owner through the transactional mail provider. Never to GitHub: reports may hold personal details and the repo will be public.

**Mark and favicon.** `src/app/icon.svg`, `favicon.ico` (the mark drawn at 16, 32 and 48 for browsers that ignore SVG icons, Safari among them), `apple-icon.png` and `src/components/Logo.tsx`: a route line arriving at a numbered stop on an ink square; the wordmark "Footnote" in Fraunces with its own superscript ¹.

**Design brief.** Tokens in `src/app/globals.css`: ink `#1E3A8A` for the route and the one accent, amber `#B5720A` for extensions, text `#141413`, muted `#5F5E5A`, paper white and a dark theme that follows the system; the map base stays light. Type: Fraunces for titles, Inter for everything else, both from `next/font`. Interest colours live in `src/lib/client/colors.ts` and differ in lightness, never hue alone. Anti-slop list: no gradients, no emoji, no identical rounded cards with uniform shadows, no colour-only distinctions, nothing thinner than 2 px on the map, real buttons and inputs with labels, focus visible, reduced motion respected, touch targets at least 44 px, sentence-case copy, no all-caps tracked labels, no meta strings joined with middle dots, no "Word — fragment" labels. Motion rules (from Apple's fluid-interface talks, Vercel's web interface guidelines and Emil Kowalski's animation notes): feedback on the press, not the release (buttons dip 3% at pointer-down); only transform and opacity animate; most transitions under 300 ms with ease-out; one orchestrated moment per screen (the wait's trails and the pins landing, the route drawing itself) and nothing else moving on its own; the next phone question slides in from the direction of travel; dialogs arrive from their button; inputs are 16 px so phones do not zoom; numbers that line up use tabular figures; headings balance their lines. The idle home map shows the cities we know well as pins; a tap starts there.

**Trial and passes.** The first walk is real and exportable, signed in or not; sign-in (email, a six-digit code or a confirm-on-tap link, no password) is asked when the person wants to keep it, and a walk built before signing in is claimed into the account. Then a week pass for travellers (one payment, seven days, no renewal) and a monthly plan for locals (renews, cancel any time from the account), each "up to N walks" with N set by environment from measured cost, never "unlimited". A walk served from the cache is free; tailoring counts; re-checks and around me are free. The passes page says why a walk costs money: what one walk costs us in computing and services (`WALK_COST_LABEL`, an all-in figure kept true to measured cost, never a raw token price) and what the rest of a pass pays for. Short privacy and terms pages exist as drafts for the test period.

## Run and test

Everything runs in containers. Nothing is installed on any host machine.

- Start the test environment: `docker compose up -d --build` (add `-V` after dependency changes). App on :3002, Mailpit (catches all email) on :8025.
- Browser tests: `tests/e2e/` with Playwright, run in CI in the Playwright container (`checks` workflow, `e2e` job) against a dev server without database or model, mocking the API routes; locally against the test environment with `BASE_URL=http://<test-host>:3002 pnpm test:e2e` from the Playwright image. The QA agent runs them too.
- Checks: `docker compose exec -T app pnpm test`, `docker compose exec -T app pnpm lint` and `docker compose exec -T app sh -c "pnpm exec next typegen && pnpm exec tsc --noEmit"` (typegen creates the route types tsc needs; the dev server does it implicitly).
- Working loop: edit in the main checkout, sync the tree to the test machine (`rsync -a --delete --exclude .git --exclude node_modules --exclude .next --exclude .env --exclude data --exclude 'public/maplibre-gl-*.mjs' ./ <test-host>:<path>/`), the dev container reloads.
- Secrets live only in `.env` (copy of `.env.example`, chmod 600, never committed). Container data lives in `./data/` (gitignored).
- CI on every PR (`.github/workflows/`): `checks` builds the dev image and runs lint, type-check and a production build inside it; `privacy` runs `.githooks/privacy-scan` over the PR's diff, messages, title and body with the denylist from the `PRIVACY_DENYLIST` secret, plus gitleaks; `review` and `qa` run independent Opus agents on the Claude Code GitHub Action, authenticated by the `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` secret (read-only reviewer; QA may run Docker and curl). Merge only when all are green. Dependabot opens weekly grouped update PRs.
- The floating Next dev badge is off (`devIndicators: false`): it reads as part of the product to testers; errors reach the browser console and the container log.
- Opening the dev server by hostname needs `DEV_ORIGINS=<host>` in `.env` (Next blocks dev resources from other origins and nothing hydrates); a changed `.env` needs `docker compose up -d app`, not `restart`.
- Model access is one OpenAI-compatible endpoint, `LLM_BASE_URL`: locally a proxy on the test host, in production LiteLLM with a real API key.
- The owner's dashboard: `/admin` exists only where `ADMIN_UI=on` (the test machine), opened with `ADMIN_TOKEN`; the public instance answers 404. It reads this instance's database, or the production mirror when `ADMIN_DATABASE_URL` points at `footnote_mirror`: `PROD_SSH=user@host scripts/pull-prod.sh` on the owner's machine dumps production over SSH (the owner's machine opens the connection; production never connects back and no database port is opened there) and restores it into the mirror. Model prices for the cost figures: `LLM_PRICE_IN_PER_M`, `LLM_PRICE_OUT_PER_M`.
- Sign-in emails land in Mailpit on :8025 (`GET /api/v1/messages` gives the code). Payments need Stripe test keys and two price ids in `.env` (`STRIPE_*`); webhooks reach the test environment through the Stripe CLI run as a container on the compose network: `docker run --rm --network footnote_default -e STRIPE_API_KEY=sk_test_... stripe/stripe-cli listen --forward-to app:3000/api/billing/webhook` (its signing secret goes in `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`). Without keys the passes page shows the offers and says payments are not switched on.

## Rules

1. **No personal information in the repo, ever.** No real names, addresses, phone numbers, postcodes, emails, home coordinates, real trips or planned destinations anywhere: code, comments, tests, fixtures, commit messages, PR titles and bodies. Test scenarios use the fixed fixture city under `tests/fixtures/`. Personal experiments go in `.local/` (gitignored). `.githooks/privacy-scan` enforces this at commit time and in CI; keep `.local/privacy-denylist.txt` current on every machine that commits. A deliberate exception is marked by `privacy-ok` on the line.
2. **Every change is a pull request**, squash-merged by the owner, titled like a conventional commit (`feat:`, `fix:`, `chore:`, `docs:`, `test:`, `refactor:`, `ci:`, `perf:`). Direct pushes to main are blocked by `.githooks/pre-push`. Enable hooks once per checkout: `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`.
3. **Small, clear changes.** PR body: what, why, how tested. No long explanations.
4. **Update this file in the same PR** as any change of plan, structure or decision. The reviewer agent checks that.
5. **Claude curates, never invents.** Stops come from candidate IDs, stories only from supplied facts, output is schema-validated, unknown IDs are rejected, routing is deterministic.

## Architecture (current)

- `src/app/` — Next.js App Router; `api/health` for checks; `api/walk/demo` builds a walk from query parameters without a model.
- `src/lib/geo.ts` — distances, detour, ordering along an axis or around a centre, search bounding box.
- `src/lib/db/` — Drizzle schema (`walks`, `events`, `feedback`), client (null without `DATABASE_URL`: the app still plans, nothing is stored), `lifecycle.ts` (migrations from `drizzle/` at server start via `src/instrumentation.ts`; daily pruning: events 90 days, the request's free text 90 days, feedback a year). `pnpm db:generate` writes a migration after a schema change; commit it.
- `src/lib/walks.ts` + `src/app/w/[id]` + `src/app/api/walk/[id]` — every built walk is stored under a short id and served as a page and as JSON. `src/app/api/events` (client beacons, validated, no cookies) and `src/app/api/feedback` (the button; email notification once the mail provider exists). `src/lib/log.ts` writes every product event to stdout and to the events table.
- `src/lib/scout/` — the scout layer. `index.ts`: for one city, search nine themes through SearXNG (`providers/search.ts`), read the pages (`providers/readpage.ts`), have the cheap model extract named places with a one-line reason, aggregate across pages, pin each to a real map place with a bounded geocode, store in `cities` and `spots` (facts and links only, never an author's text). `spots.ts`: spots inside a walk's box become candidates with a credited fact (`Recommended on "<source>": <reason>`), a score boost and `sources` the page links. Triggered by `POST /api/admin/scout` with the `ADMIN_TOKEN`; a scheduler and the per-city cache of pre-made walks come next.
- City cache (`src/lib/scout/places.ts`, `presets.ts`, `scheduler.ts`): a city's map places are fetched once for its core (at most 6 km from the centre) and kept in `places` for a week; a walk whose start lies in a fresh city reads candidates from there and never waits on the public places server; otherwise it goes live and registers the city (`cities`, status pending). The scheduler (`src/instrumentation.ts`, every six hours, `SCHEDULER=off` to disable) caches one due city's places and builds its three preset walks, and scouts one due city. `GET /api/cities/[id]` lists a city's presets; `POST /api/walk/[id]/refresh` re-checks a stored walk's arrivals, hours and weather for a date with no model; `POST /api/walk/[id]/tailor` rebuilds it from the person's changes using the cache, streaming like `POST /api/walk`. `pipeline/stream.ts` is the shared streaming builder.
- Accounts and billing: `src/lib/auth/` (`normalise.ts` email key: lower-case, plus-tag off, Gmail dots and alias folded, throwaway domains refused; `codes.ts` six-digit code, link token, peppered hashes; `index.ts` start, verify by code, verify by link; `session.ts` the one HttpOnly cookie, 90 days, `currentUser()` after `connection()`), `src/lib/mail.ts` (SMTP through nodemailer; Mailpit locally), `src/lib/ratelimit.ts` (in-memory sliding counters: codes per address and per hashed IP, anonymous builds per hashed IP), `src/lib/account/allowance.ts` (`decide`: trial walks per browser id or per account, a pass's walks within its period; `pipeline/stream.ts` gates there after the cache check and stores the owner), `src/lib/billing/stripe.ts` (the two offers, Checkout, the customer portal, the webhook applied once per event id: week pass = seven days from payment, monthly = the subscription's period and status). Routes under `api/auth/` (start, verify, link, signout, me, claim) and `api/billing/` (checkout, portal, webhook). Screens: `/signin`, `/signin/link`, `/account` (plan, walks left, kept walks, billing portal, sign out), `/plans`, `/privacy`, `/terms`; `components/account/` (the header chip, sign-in, link continue, plan button, keep-this-walk, account actions). The planner shows a limit panel when the stream answers `error` with `code: "limit"`.
- Dashboard (`src/app/admin/`, `src/lib/admin/`, `src/components/admin/`): `gate.ts` (enabled only with `ADMIN_UI=on`; a cookie holding the hashed token, set by `api/admin/login`, thirty days; `requireAdmin()` after `connection()`), `db.ts` (reads the mirror or the own database), `queries.ts` (event counts, tokens and cost, wait times, day by day, feedback with done marks, people with walk counts, recent walks, events by type, cities with spot and place counts), `cost.ts`. Pages: overview, feedback (done marks in the local `admin_marks` table so they survive a fresh pull), people (Stripe customer links), walks, events (filter by type), cities (scout now, one scheduler round, through `api/admin/scout` with the cookie). `scripts/pull-prod.sh` fills the mirror.
- UI (`src/components/`): `request/RequestForm.tsx` (the six questions, one per screen on phones, all on one page with the live summary sentence on desktop), `request/WaitPanel.tsx` (four honest stages with real timings, a rotating fact, a note when slow), `request/Planner.tsx` (the home screen: form beside the map; streams `POST /api/walk`, fills the map with the search box, candidates and chosen pins, then opens `/w/[id]`), `walk/WalkView.tsx` (the walk page: route with casing, numbered stops with interest halos, time pills per leg, closed badge, weather pill, stop cards with hook, story, why and sources, Google Maps, GPX, copy link, tailor, re-check for a date), `Feedback.tsx` (the bug button), `map/WalkMap.tsx` (MapLibre on OpenFreeMap tiles; its worker and shared modules are copied to `public/` by `predev`/`prebuild`, and the sync must exclude them). `/tailor?of=<id>` prefills the form from a stored request and rebuilds through the cache. Client helpers in `src/lib/client/` (SSE reader, event beacons with a browser-made session id, colours).
- `src/lib/providers/openmeteo.ts` — keyless forecast for the walk's window, summarised to one sentence and a nudge for the curator. `src/lib/pipeline/hours.ts` — OSM opening hours read for the walk's window (a timed place closed throughout is dropped before curation; hours become a citable fact), arrival time per stop after routing, open-or-closed note.
- `src/lib/providers/` — `types.ts` (Geocoder, PlacesProvider, Router, Candidate), `cache.ts` (TTL cache, per-provider throttle, User-Agent), `nominatim.ts`, `overpass.ts` (candidates with facts from tags), `osrm.ts` (foot profile). All dev-grade public services, swapped before going public.
- `src/lib/pipeline/` — `intent.ts` (Intent schema, interest → OSM selectors, pace, dwell), `candidates.ts` (score, dedupe, pick and order), `fit.ts` (deterministic time-budget fitting), `export.ts` (Google Maps link with the nine-waypoint cap, GPX), `walk.ts` (orchestration).
- `tests/unit/` with vitest; `tests/fixtures/city.ts` is the only place with coordinates (central Bath).
- `compose.yml` + `Dockerfile` — dev target with the source bind-mounted; a production target comes with the cloud deployment.
- `src/lib/llm/client.ts` — the one module that talks to the model: OpenAI-compatible chat, JSON answers validated with Zod, one corrective retry, usage captured. `src/lib/pipeline/interpret.ts` (six-question form → Intent, the model only reads the free-text line), `curate.ts` (stops chosen from candidate ids with cited facts; no facts, no stop), `plan.ts` (the staged pipeline with events), `src/app/api/walk` (POST, server-sent events: stage, walk, error). `src/lib/log.ts` writes JSON-line events with the free text scrubbed; a Postgres events table follows.

## Decisions

- 2026-10-31 — Scout layer built on self-hosted SearXNG plus page reading plus extraction by the cheap model; Instagram has no usable access, and blogs, travel sites and forums carry the same recommendations. Spots are stored with a one-line reason and source links only. Unmatched names are dropped: no invented places.
- 2026-10-31 — Product logs in our own Postgres with no cookies and no third parties; free text kept 90 days. Interface and stories in the person's language from day one.
- 2026-10-31 — Product reshaped after two independent evaluations and the owner's review: a plan-builder for dates and companions, not a mood walk. Adopted: the six-question request, the scout layer with a per-city cache of pre-made walks, "no story, no stop", opening hours and weather in selection, companions as a pace profile, the map and waiting design, stories as one expandable line, walks as shareable pages, the footnoted walk, "around me" on the phone, a real exportable trial with sign-in afterwards. See Product.
- 2026-10-31 — Dependabot handles minors and patches only; majors are moved by hand (Node to the next LTS, and TypeScript, ESLint and Next together, since typescript-eslint lags TypeScript majors). Node 25+ ships no corepack, so pnpm is installed with npm at the version pinned in package.json. Dependabot PRs are merged only when checks are green.
- 2026-10-31 — Repo is private under GitHub Free, which has no branch protection: a pre-push hook blocks pushes to main until the repo goes public.
- 2026-10-31 — Everything in containers. Test environment on a home server, production on a cloud VM with its own LiteLLM. The app only ever sees an OpenAI-compatible base URL.
- 2026-10-30 — Open core: the app is public (licence to decide before release, AGPL favoured); curated city data, billing and deployment config stay private.
- 2026-10-30 — Login by magic link plus a short code, no passwords. The free trial also requires sign-in; trial abuse is limited by email normalisation (plus-tags, Gmail dots, throwaway domains) and device/IP limits.
- 2026-10-30 — The free trial shows one full walk with locked actions. Paid access is a subscription sized from measured cost, advertised as "up to N walks", never "unlimited". Stripe. Paid access opens in the UK and US first.
- 2026-10-30 — Data: OSM, Wikidata, Wikipedia, photo density (Commons, Flickr), film locations (Wikidata), shops and museums (OSM, Foursquare open places), blogger recommendations via a cached per-city scout job that stores source links. Routing and geocoding through hosted APIs.
- 2026-10-30 — CI: an independent Opus reviewer (read-only) and an Opus QA agent on every PR via the Claude Code GitHub Action; the privacy scan with a denylist kept outside the repo; gitleaks.
- 2026-10-30 — UK compliance from day one: UK GDPR (minimal data, no request text in logs), PECR (essential storage only), consumer law (digital-content waiver at checkout, renewal reminders, easy cancellation), ODbL and CC BY-SA attribution, WCAG 2.2 AA.

- 2026-11-01 — Sign-in is a six-digit code typed on the device or a link that needs one tap on the page it opens (mail scanners open links). One essential cookie, nothing else. The trial is counted per browser id for strangers (plus a daily cap per hashed IP) and per account once signed in; a walk claimed into an account counts there. Passes: a week pass is one payment for seven days (travellers do not want a renewal), the monthly plan is a Stripe subscription; "up to N walks" comes from the environment so the number can follow measured cost. Stripe holds prices and cards; we hold a customer id and the plan state. Feedback is emailed to `FEEDBACK_TO` through the same SMTP.

- 2026-11-01 — The owner's dashboard lives only on the owner's machine, never on the public instance, and reads a mirror of production that the owner's machine pulls over SSH. Production never initiates a connection and exposes no database port. The passes page tells people what a walk costs to make and what the rest of a pass pays for, with an all-in figure kept true to measured cost.
